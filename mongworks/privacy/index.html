Privacy & support

MongWorks

MongWorks is a preventative bike-maintenance app. It projects a bike's component wear from a member's real riding data via Strava, and prompts service at the right time.

Data we collect

To do this, MongWorks collects and stores:

  • Strava data — via Strava's OAuth connection (scope read, profile:read_all, activity:read_all), we read your ride/activity data (distance, elevation, timing, and location of rides) and your registered gear (bike names), used to attribute wear to the correct bike.
  • Account data — your email address and authentication details, held by our backend provider (Supabase). Supabase is also where the mailing list described below is stored — it is the same database, not a separate service.
  • Service Log photos — optional documentation photos you attach when logging a service or inspection.
  • A notification token — if you allow notifications, your device issues a token identifying where to deliver them, and we store it so we can tell you when a part is due for service. It identifies the installation of the app, not you, and turning notifications off in your device settings stops it being used.
  • Postcode — used only to estimate shipping cost/time when comparing parts prices; not a full address, not device location.

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for advertising — MongWorks has no ad or analytics SDKs.

Who else sees it

Strava is where the riding data comes from, and Supabase is where everything we keep is stored. Three features cannot work without asking another company a question, so for each of those a specific piece of your data goes to a specific place:

  • OpenWeather — the coordinates and time of each ride. Wear depends heavily on what you rode through, so for every ride we look up the weather at that place and hour. This is the most revealing thing we send anyone: it is where and when you were riding. It goes without your email or anything else tying it to your account, and we never store it — there is no column for a coordinate anywhere in our database. Ride locations are read from Strava, used for the lookup, and let go.
  • eBay — your postcode, attached to the request for parts prices. That is what makes the shipping cost and delivery window specific to where you are rather than a national average. Your postcode is not sent to any other retailer.
  • Expo — the notification token and the text of the notification, because Expo is the service that delivers push messages to your device. Expo issues that token in the first place, and hands each message on to Google's or Apple's push service to get it to your phone, so those see it in passing too. Be aware what the message itself contains: it names the bike as you named it, and says something on it is due for service. That is the entire message — but if you have put your own name in a bike's nickname, it travels with it. The app also asks Expo on launch whether an update is available, which shows Expo your IP address and app version.

When MongWorks compares parts prices it searches retailers for the part in question: today that is eBay, and Bicycles Online through the affiliate network Impact.com. An Amazon integration exists in the code but is switched off; if it is ever turned on it will receive the same thing the others do. Those searches carry the name of the part and nothing about you, with the single exception of the postcode sent to eBay above.

Beyond what each of those bullets describes, none of these companies receives your email address, your Strava identity, or your service history. We have no agreement with any of them to share your data, and we do not sell it to them or to anyone else.

One thing worth saying plainly, because it explains why we talk to retailers at all: the links MongWorks shows you are affiliate links, so if you buy a part through one, we may earn a commission. It costs you nothing and it does not change which parts we tell you are worn — but you should know the incentive exists.

The mailing list

MongWorks hasn't launched. If you gave us your email address on the welcome page to hear when it does, that address sits in a mailing list which is separate from the app: you don't have a MongWorks account yet, and joining the list doesn't create one.

When you submit that form, we record five things:

  • Your email address — so there is somewhere to send the announcement.
  • The date and time you submitted it — the record of when you agreed to hear from us.
  • Your IP address — part of that same record, and what we count to limit how often the form can be submitted from one place.
  • Your browser's user agent — the identifying string your browser sends with the request.
  • The page you came from — the referring URL, if your browser sent one.

The last four exist to show the sign-up was genuine and freely given. They cannot be reconstructed after the fact, which is why they are captured at the moment you submit rather than later.

Alongside those we store a fixed label naming which page the form was on, so a second sign-up form added later can be told apart from this one. It describes the page, not you.

This list is for marketing, and for nothing else. We will use it to send you one email, on the day there is something to download. It is never joined to app data, it is not used to target anything, and it is not shared with or sold to anyone.

We don't send a confirmation email, because nothing in MongWorks sends email yet. That means we cannot prove an address was entered by the person who owns it — so if someone has put yours in without asking, tell us and we will take it off.

Coming off the list

There is no unsubscribe link, because there is no mail going out to put one in. Until there is, coming off the list is an email request: write to info@mong-rat.com asking to be removed from the MongWorks mailing list, and we will delete the record — address, timestamp, IP and all.

When there is finally an email to send, it will carry a working unsubscribe link of its own.

These web pages

Separately from the app, this page and the MongWorks welcome page are ordinary web pages, and two things are worth saying about them.

  • We set no cookies and run no analytics. We do not measure or record your visit, and nothing you do while reading reaches us — the sign-up form above is the only thing on either page that sends us anything.
  • Two other companies see the request. The pages are hosted on GitHub Pages, and they load the IBM Plex Mono typeface from Google Fonts. Simply opening a page therefore makes requests to GitHub and to Google which carry your IP address and your browser's user agent, and both keep their own server logs. That is a property of how the pages are hosted rather than something we ask for: we never see those logs, and nothing from them reaches us or gets stored by us.

Requesting account deletion

To delete your MongWorks account and the data listed above, email info@mong-rat.com from the address associated with your account, asking us to delete it.

Once we process the request, we will:

  • Revoke your Strava connection directly with Strava (not just forget it on our side)
  • Delete your Bikes, Components, Service Log, and Gear Mappings
  • Delete your Service Log photos
  • Delete your stored postcode and your notification tokens
  • Delete your account and sign-in details

Any Bike/Component catalog corrections you contributed that have already been shared with other members stay in place — they aren't personally identifiable, and removing them would break other members' bikes that rely on the same shared reference data.

The mailing list is a separate request, because it is separate data — see above. If you are on both and want both gone, say so and we will do both.

Support

Using MongWorks with a connected Strava account and have a question or ran into a problem — with the Strava connection, your bikes, or anything else in the app? Email info@mong-rat.com and we'll help.
